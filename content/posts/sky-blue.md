Sunlight looks white, but it's actually a mix of all the colours of the rainbow. The reason the sky looks blue comes down to how that light interacts with air.

## Light is made of different wavelengths

Each colour of light has a different wavelength. Red light has long waves; blue and violet light have short waves.

## Air molecules scatter short waves more

When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it hits tiny nitrogen and oxygen molecules. These molecules scatter shorter wavelengths much more strongly than longer ones. This effect is called **Rayleigh scattering**.

So blue light gets bounced around the sky in every direction. Wherever you look, some of that scattered blue light reaches your eyes.

## Then why isn't the sky violet?

Violet light scatters even more than blue. But:

1. The Sun emits less violet light than blue.
2. Some violet light is absorbed high in the atmosphere.
3. Our eyes are more sensitive to blue than violet.

Put together, our brain sees the sky as blue.

## Why sunsets are orange and red

At sunrise and sunset, sunlight travels through much more atmosphere to reach you. By the time it arrives, most of the blue has been scattered away, leaving the longer red, orange, and yellow wavelengths.

## Bonus fact: Mars has blue sunsets

On Mars, fine dust scatters light differently. Daytime skies look butterscotch, while sunsets glow blue — the opposite of Earth.

Next time someone asks why the sky is blue, you can tell them: it's sunlight being scattered by the air itself.
