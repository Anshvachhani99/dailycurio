Open any streaming app and you'll see rows of shows picked "for you." Behind those rows is a recommendation system that is constantly learning from your behaviour.

## What the app tracks

- What you watch, and **how much** of it you finish
- What you search for
- What time of day you watch, and on which device
- What you scroll past without clicking
- Ratings, likes, and "not interested" signals

## The two main approaches

**Collaborative filtering** finds people with similar viewing habits. If thousands of people who loved Show A also loved Show B, you'll probably be shown Show B.

**Content-based filtering** looks at the show itself — genre, actors, mood, pace — and suggests similar titles.

Most platforms combine both, along with machine learning models that predict how likely you are to press play.

## Even the thumbnail is personalised

Many services test different artwork for the same show. If you often watch romantic comedies, you might see a thumbnail featuring the couple. A thriller fan might see a tense close-up from the same film.

## Why your homepage changes so often

The system is always running small experiments: which row order keeps you watching, which titles you ignore, and what you come back to. Every session updates its picture of you.

## How to "train" your recommendations

1. Use separate profiles for each person in the house.
2. Rate or "like" shows you enjoyed.
3. Remove titles from "Continue watching" that you've abandoned.
4. Use the "not interested" option when you see it.

Algorithms aren't perfect, but a few small actions can make your homepage feel a lot more like *you*.
