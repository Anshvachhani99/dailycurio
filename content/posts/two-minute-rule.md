Procrastination isn't usually about laziness. It's about the feeling that a task is too big to start. The 2-minute rule shrinks that feeling.

## Part 1: If it takes less than two minutes, do it now

Replying to a quick message, washing a cup, filing a document — these tiny tasks pile up and create mental clutter. Doing them immediately is faster than remembering them later.

## Part 2: Start any new habit with a two-minute version

Want to read more? Read one page. Want to exercise? Put on your workout clothes. Want to write? Open the document and write one sentence.

The goal isn't to stop after two minutes. The goal is to make starting so easy that you can't say no.

## Why it works

- **Starting is the hardest part.** Once you begin, momentum takes over.
- **It builds identity.** Each small win tells your brain, "I'm the kind of person who does this."
- **It removes perfectionism.** Two minutes can't be perfect, so you stop worrying about it.

## Examples you can use today

| Big goal | Two-minute version |
| --- | --- |
| Get fit | Do 5 squats |
| Learn a language | Review 3 flashcards |
| Keep a clean home | Clear one surface |
| Save money | Check your account balance |
| Meditate | Take 10 slow breaths |

## Make it stick

Attach your two-minute habit to something you already do: "After I make my morning tea, I'll read one page." This is called **habit stacking**, and it pairs perfectly with the 2-minute rule.

Start small. Stay consistent. The results will grow on their own.
