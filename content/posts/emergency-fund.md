An emergency fund is money set aside only for unexpected events. It's the foundation of every good financial plan — before investing, before big purchases.

## How much should you save?

A common guideline is **3 to 6 months of essential expenses**.

- **3 months** if you have a stable job, no dependents, and good health insurance.
- **6 months or more** if you're self-employed, have irregular income, or support a family.

Note: it's based on *expenses*, not salary. If you need ₹35,000 a month for rent, food, bills, and EMIs, a 6-month fund is ₹2,10,000.

## Where to keep it

Your emergency fund should be **safe and easy to access**, not invested for high returns.

- A separate high-interest savings account
- A sweep-in fixed deposit
- A liquid fund (for part of it)

Avoid keeping it in stocks — markets can fall exactly when you need the money.

## How to build it without stress

1. **Start with a mini goal:** ₹10,000 or one month of expenses.
2. **Automate a fixed amount** every salary day.
3. **Add windfalls:** bonuses, tax refunds, and gifts.
4. **Keep it separate** so you aren't tempted to spend it.

## What counts as an emergency?

- ✅ Job loss, medical bills, urgent home or vehicle repairs
- ❌ Sales, holidays, a new phone you simply want

## Refill after you use it

If you dip into it, pause other non-essential savings goals until it's back to the target.

An emergency fund won't make you rich, but it will make you calm — and that's worth a lot.
