The "10,000 steps" target actually started as a marketing slogan for a pedometer in the 1960s. But modern research shows that walking more really does change your health.

## Your heart gets stronger

Regular brisk walking improves blood pressure and cholesterol levels. Large studies have found that people who walk more each day have a lower risk of heart disease.

## Your mood improves

A 20–30 minute walk can reduce stress and anxiety. Walking outdoors, especially in green spaces, gives an extra boost.

## Your blood sugar stays steadier

A short walk after meals helps your muscles use glucose, which can reduce sugar spikes. Even 10 minutes after dinner helps.

## Your joints and bones benefit

Walking is low-impact, so it strengthens bones and keeps joints mobile without the stress of running.

## Your sleep gets better

People who are more active during the day tend to fall asleep faster and sleep more deeply.

## Do you really need 10,000?

Not necessarily. Research suggests that the biggest benefits start around **7,000–8,000 steps** for most adults, with gains continuing beyond that. If you're at 3,000 now, adding 1,000–2,000 is a great start.

## Easy ways to add more steps

- Take phone calls while walking.
- Use the stairs for one or two floors.
- Get off the bus or metro one stop early.
- Walk for 10 minutes after each meal.
- Make one weekend plan a walking plan — a market, park, or heritage area.

*This article is for general information only and is not medical advice. Talk to a doctor before starting a new exercise routine if you have a health condition.*
