Budgeting doesn't have to mean tracking every single rupee. The **50/30/20 rule** gives you a simple framework that works for most people.

## The rule in one line

Split your take-home pay into:

- **50% for needs** — rent, groceries, bills, transport, loan EMIs
- **30% for wants** — eating out, shopping, subscriptions, trips
- **20% for savings and investing** — emergency fund, SIPs, retirement

## A real example

Say your monthly take-home salary is **₹60,000**.

| Category | Share | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs | 50% | ₹30,000 |
| Wants | 30% | ₹18,000 |
| Savings | 20% | ₹12,000 |

That ₹12,000 a month adds up to ₹1,44,000 a year — before any returns.

## What if your needs are more than 50%?

In big cities, rent alone can eat up a large share. That's okay. Adjust to something like **60/20/20** and protect the savings slice first. The goal is consistency, not perfection.

## How to make it automatic

1. **Pay yourself first.** Set up an auto-transfer for your savings on salary day.
2. **Use two accounts.** One for needs and bills, one for spending money.
3. **Review once a month.** Fifteen minutes is enough to see where things went.

## Common mistakes

- Counting a credit card bill as "wants" when it was actually groceries.
- Forgetting yearly costs like insurance premiums — divide them by 12.
- Skipping savings "just this month" — it quietly becomes every month.

The 50/30/20 rule isn't magic, but it gives every rupee a job. Start this month and adjust as you learn.
